Where the Volta meets the sea

This is Ada.

At the mouth of the Volta, Ada holds one of West Africa's great salt lagoons, a shoreline the ocean is steadily taking back, and a fishing economy under strain. Every one of those is a policy question — and every one of them has been treated as somebody else's problem for too long.

Songor

One of Ghana's largest salt-producing lagoons sits in our constituency — and the question of who benefits from it is still unsettled.

The Estuary

Where the Volta River meets the Atlantic at Ada Foah — a fishery, a tourism asset, and a shoreline in retreat.

Asafotufiami

Our annual August festival brings Ada home from across Ghana and the diaspora. It should also bring investment.

Ada East

Ada Foah as district capital, with communities stretching inland through Kasseh, Big Ada and Koluedor.

The Candidate

Frank Quarshie
Djagbletey

Djagbletey is a son of Ada who came back. Not for a season, not for a campaign — to live here, work here, and be answerable here. The name on the seal is a family name, and in Ada a family name is a promise you can be held to.

This candidacy rests on a simple proposition: Ada does not need a representative who visits. It needs one who returns, who rebuilds what has been left to fall, and who represents Ada in Parliament as fiercely as Ada deserves.

The Ada Charter

Six commitments.
Written to be checked.

Not a wish list. Six areas where an MP for Ada has real leverage — through the floor of Parliament, the committee system, the constituency development fund, and the plain work of showing up.

01

Songor: salt is a birthright

The Songor lagoon has sustained Ada families for generations. Any arrangement over it must put the atsiakpo — the small-scale winners — first.

  • Push for full public disclosure of Songor lease and licence terms
  • Protected, enforceable access for community salt winners
  • A binding local-benefit and royalty framework Ada can audit
  • Independent environmental monitoring of the lagoon
02

Hold the shoreline

Totope, Azizanya, Ada Foah — the sea has taken homes, classrooms and burial grounds. Sea defence is not a favour to Ada. It is overdue.

  • Sustained pressure for the next phases of the Ada sea defence works
  • Proper resettlement terms for displaced families — in writing
  • Coastal monitoring so we argue from evidence, not from grief
  • Mangrove and dune restoration with community labour
03

The estuary economy

Fishing at the Volta mouth is harder every year. Our fisherfolk need cold storage, fair credit, and rules that are actually enforced on everyone.

  • Cold chain and landing-site upgrades at Ada Foah and Azizanya
  • Outboard motor and gear support that reaches real fishermen
  • Backing for fish processors — the women who carry this trade
  • Serious enforcement against light fishing and undersized nets
04

Ada as a destination

Visitors already come for the estuary, the beaches and Asafotufiami. Almost none of that value stays in Ada. That is a design failure we can fix.

  • Ada tourism corridor: roads, signage, sanitation, safety
  • Asafotufiami developed as a national-calibre festival event
  • Licensing that favours Ada-owned guesthouses, boats and guides
  • Youth training in hospitality, boat safety and guiding
05

Work for young Ada

Our best young people leave for Accra because Ada offers them nothing to stay for. Reverse that, and every other problem gets easier.

  • An Ada skills and digital work hub with real connectivity
  • Apprenticeship placements tied to local employers
  • Start-up capital for salt, fish, farm and tourism ventures
  • Transparent, published allocation of constituency funds
06

The basics, done properly

Water you can drink. A clinic that is open. A road that survives the rains. Ada has waited a long time for the ordinary things.

  • Potable water for communities living with saline groundwater
  • Staffing, supplies and referral transport for our health facilities
  • Feeder roads maintained on a published schedule
  • Classrooms, furniture and teacher housing where they are short
