Where the Volta meets the sea

This is Ada.

At the mouth of the Volta, Ada farms hard, saline ground, keeps schools that are short of teachers, and trains young people who then leave for Accra — all beside a great salt lagoon and a shoreline the ocean is taking back. Every one of those is a policy question — and every one of them has been treated as somebody else's problem for too long.

His Story

Ada remembers its children.

It remembers them in the rhythm of the Volta River, in the whisper of the estuary winds, in the warm sands of Ada Foah where stories settle like grains of time. It remembers them in the laughter of markets, in the songs of festivals, and in the quiet prayers of elders who speak the names of those who carry Ada's spirit across oceans.

Among those names, one rises with familiarity and pride: Djagbletey.

Born of Ada's soil, shaped by its traditions, and carried by the legacy of parents, grandparents and courageous ancestors whose footprints still echo through Big Ada, Kasseh, Azizanya, Totope, Otrokper, Koluedor, Anyamam, Pute, Luhuose, Wasakuse, Matsekope, Kewunor, Goi, Alorkpem, Salom, Tojeh, Sege, Hwakpo, Kaja-nya and Tamatoku, Frank Quarshie's story begins where Ada's heart beats strongest — in community.

For more than twenty years, Frank journeyed far from home, crossing the Atlantic to build a life in the United States. There, he learned the discipline of leadership, the patience of service, and the art of guiding people toward shared purpose. He became a steward of teams, a builder of systems, a cultivator of trust. Yet even as he grew, Ada's call followed him — soft, steady, unbroken.

“ With a lineage of strong community builders, my maternal and paternal grandparents have been pillars of the Ada community for generations. I am here to follow in those exact same footsteps — to continue what they have always done, and to help propel Ada and the rest of our towns and communities into the future for the next generation. — Frank Quarshie Djagbletey
Because the river remembers its tide.

Now, Frank returns to Ada not as a stranger, but as a son guided by memory and mission. He returns with the humility of his lineage and the strength of his experience. He returns with a promise carried in his campaign's own heartbeat: Return. Rebuild. Represent.

He returns to honour the ancestors whose names shaped him. He returns to rebuild the communities whose hopes raised him. He returns to represent the people whose spirit defines him.

As a member and affiliate of the National Democratic Congress, Frank steps forward with a vision woven from Ada's needs and Ada's dreams:

  • Economic revival that restores dignity and opportunity
  • Education that lights the path for every child
  • Governance that listens, respects, and protects
  • Community development rooted in tradition and lifted by innovation
  • Tourism that celebrates Ada's beauty — its waters, its culture, its soul

The Ada Charter

Eight commitments.
Written to be checked.

Not a wish list. Eight areas where an MP for Ada has real leverage — through the floor of Parliament, the committee system, the constituency development fund, and the plain work of showing up. See where each one stands →

01

Farming that pays

Ada farms on hard ground — saline soils, an unreliable rain season, and buyers who set the price. Farming here fails on inputs, water and markets, not on effort.

  • Irrigation and boreholes for farming communities off the Volta
  • Fertiliser, seed and mechanisation support that reaches smallholders
  • Storage and aggregation so a harvest is not sold at a loss
  • Extension officers posted to Ada, and actually present
02

Educate every Ada child

A child in Ada should not start school already behind a child in Accra. Classrooms, teachers and the cost of staying in school are where that gap is decided.

  • Classrooms, furniture and teacher housing where they are short
  • Incentives to post and keep trained teachers in coastal and island schools
  • Support for the costs that push children out — fees, books, transport
  • Scholarships and TVET places for Ada students continuing past SHS
03

Work for Ada's Youth

Our best young people leave for Accra because Ada offers them nothing to stay for. Reverse that, and every other problem gets easier.

  • An Ada skills and digital work hub with real connectivity
  • Apprenticeship placements tied to local employers
  • Start-up capital for salt, fish, farm and tourism ventures
  • Transparent, published allocation of constituency funds
04

Songor: salt is a birthright

The Songor lagoon has sustained Ada families for generations. Any arrangement over it must put the atsiakpo — the small-scale winners — first.

  • Push for full public disclosure of Songor lease and licence terms
  • Protected, enforceable access for community salt winners
  • A binding local-benefit and royalty framework Ada can audit
  • Independent environmental monitoring of the lagoon
05

Hold the shoreline

Totope, Azizanya, Ada Foah — the sea has taken homes, classrooms and burial grounds. Sea defence is not a favour to Ada. It is overdue.

  • Sustained pressure for the next phases of the Ada sea defence works
  • Proper resettlement terms for displaced families — in writing
  • Coastal monitoring so we argue from evidence, not from grief
  • Mangrove and dune restoration with community labour
06

The estuary economy

Fishing at the Volta mouth is harder every year. Our fisherfolk need cold storage, fair credit, and rules that are actually enforced on everyone.

  • Cold chain and landing-site upgrades at Ada Foah and Azizanya
  • Outboard motor and gear support that reaches real fishermen
  • Backing for fish processors — the women who carry this trade
  • Serious enforcement against light fishing and undersized nets
07

Ada as a destination

Visitors already come for the estuary, the beaches and Asafotufiami. Almost none of that value stays in Ada. That is a design failure we can fix.

  • Ada tourism corridor: roads, signage, sanitation, safety
  • Asafotufiami developed as a national-calibre festival event
  • Licensing that favours Ada-owned guesthouses, boats and guides
  • Youth training in hospitality, boat safety and guiding
08

The basics, done properly

Water you can drink. A clinic that is open. A road that survives the rains. Ada has waited a long time for the ordinary things.

  • Potable water for communities living with saline groundwater
  • Staffing, supplies and referral transport for our health facilities
  • Feeder roads maintained on a published schedule
  • Street lighting and drainage in the towns that flood first
